🗺️ Reverse Outline: What Is My Draft Actually Doing?

Stop asking what you meant to write. Look at what is actually on the page.

WRITER: “My essay is organized.”
DRAFT: 😐
REVERSE OUTLINE: “We’ll see about that.”
🔓 Unwritten Rule: An outline is not only something you make before writing. You can outline a draft you already have to see what each paragraph is actually doing.

🩻 Draft X-Ray Questions

1. What is this paragraph actually doing?

2. Does that job help the thesis?

3. Is another paragraph doing the same job?

4. Is this paragraph doing too many jobs?

5. Does the reader need a bridge, move, cut, or more development?

🗺️ You Found the Actual Draft

An outline tells you what you intended to write. A reverse outline shows you what your reader actually encounters.

🩻 X-Ray My Own Draft

Choose how many paragraphs you want to map. Then give each one a short job.

🗺️ Reverse Outline Check

Do two paragraphs have the same job?
Does one paragraph have several jobs?
Does any paragraph have no clear connection to the thesis?
Does an idea disappear and return later?
Does the reader need a bridge between ideas?
Is the conclusion introducing a major idea that belongs earlier?
