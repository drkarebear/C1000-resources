Academic Triage Tool

What’s Still Possible?

When several things pile up at once, your brain may turn “I have a lot to deal with” into “There’s no point trying.”

This tool will help you sort what is actually actionable, what needs information, what can wait, and what your next move should be.

You do not have to save everything to improve your situation.

A realistic plan may include doing some things now, asking about others, and intentionally setting some things aside.

Step 1

Get It Out of Your Head

Start by listing the things that feel like they are hanging over you. Do not organize them yet.

Your brain has been trying to hold all of this at once.

Now that it is outside your head, you can sort it instead of carrying it as one giant problem.

Step 2

Not Everything on the List Is the Same

Sort what you found into three kinds of problems.

DO NOW Something is open, actionable, and worth attention.
ASK ABOUT You need information before you know what to do.
NOT NOW It is closed, less urgent, or can wait while something else matters more.
Step 3

Is It a Task or a Question?

“Make up Quiz 3” is not actually a task if you do not know whether Quiz 3 can be reopened.

The real task may be:

“Ask whether Quiz 3 is still available to make up.”

Some things feel huge because you are trying to solve a question as though it were a task.

Step 4

Find the Move With the Most Leverage

Do not automatically choose the oldest task. Ask which action would help your situation the most.

Nearest deadline: Is something due very soon?
Big impact: Is one task worth much more than another?
Unlocks something: Will doing this help you move forward on other work?
Needs another person: Should you ask a question now because you are waiting on information?
Small restart: Is there a short task that would help you regain momentum?
Do not let guilt choose your priority.

Let information choose it.

A closed 10-point assignment from two weeks ago may feel emotionally louder than a 100-point essay due tomorrow. That does not make it the better place to spend your time.

Step 5

Decide What You Are Not Doing Right Now

A realistic plan includes limits. Choosing not to work on something right now does not mean it never matters.

It means you are protecting your attention for what matters more.

Step 6

Plan for the Time You Actually Have

Do not make a plan for the time you wish you had. Choose the amount of usable time you actually have right now.

Step 7

Do You Need a Plan or Better Information?

Which description is closest to your situation?

This may be an information problem now.

Working harder cannot answer questions you do not have enough information to answer. Before making a major decision, contact the appropriate professor, counselor, or college support person so you can understand your actual options and deadlines.

Step 8

Build Your Possible Plan

Your plan does not need to fix everything. It only needs to make your situation better than it was before you started.
