Drafting reset

Good Enough to Draft

You do not need to make the whole paper good right now. You need enough material to discover what you want to say.

Start1 of 8
Before you start

Drafting and judging are different jobs.

If you try to generate an idea, organize it, word it perfectly, check grammar, sound academic, and decide whether it is good enough all at once, writing gets much harder.

For the next few minutes, your job is only to make material.

You can judge, reorganize, polish, and proofread later—when there is actually something on the page to work with.

This lab will walk you through one small decision at a time. Nothing you type is submitted anywhere.

Step 1

What are you requiring from yourself?

Choose any rules your brain seems to be giving you before you are allowed to write.

Those may be reasonable goals for a finished paper.

They are expensive requirements for a first draft.

Step 2

Give yourself temporary permission.

Choose a few things you are allowed to do while you are drafting.

This is not permission to submit weak work.

It is permission to create material that revision can work with.

Step 3

The bad first sentence challenge.

Your sentence is not allowed to be impressive.

For one minute, explain what you think you may want to say. Do not stop to fix the wording.

Need a starter? Pick one and it will appear in the box.

1:00

You can continue even if you did not use the full minute.

Step 4

Don’t ask, “Is this good?”

That question is too early. Your rough writing only needs to give you something to work with.

Ask this instead:

Is there anything here I can use?

Step 5

Use placeholders instead of stop signs.

You will sometimes reach something you cannot solve while drafting. That does not mean the whole draft needs to stop.

[SOURCE HERE]
[BETTER WORD]
[CHECK MLA]
[EXPLAIN WHY]
[CONNECT TO THESIS]
[COME BACK]
Stopping

I cannot remember the exact quote, so I stop drafting and start searching.

Keeping going

Carr argues that [ADD QUOTE HERE], which shows...

Step 6

What does your reader need next?

You have an idea now. Instead of evaluating the whole paper, make one forward-moving decision.

Your next move

Keep drafting before you start editing.

You now have material. The temptation may be to go back to the first sentence and polish it. Resist that for a little longer.

Your next job:

Keep writing for 10 minutes without editing your opening sentence.
