10-Minute Drafting Reset

Good Enough to Draft

Sometimes you are not out of ideas. You are trying to make every sentence good before you let yourself write the next one.

This tool will help you stop judging long enough to create something you can actually revise.

Drafting and judging are different jobs.

If you try to generate an idea, organize it, word it perfectly, check the grammar, sound academic, and decide whether it is good enough all at once, writing gets much harder.

For the next few minutes, your job is only to make material.

Step 1

What Are You Requiring From Yourself?

Choose any rules your brain seems to be giving you before you are allowed to write.

Those may be reasonable goals for a finished paper.

They are expensive requirements for a first draft.

Every requirement uses attention that could be helping you discover what you want to say.

Step 2

Give Yourself Temporary Permission

Choose a few things you are allowed to do for this draft.

This is not permission to submit weak work.

It is permission to create material that revision can work with.

Step 3

The Bad First Sentence Challenge

Your sentence is not allowed to be impressive.

For one minute, explain what you think you may want to say. Do not stop to fix wording.

Pick a starter if you need one:

1:00
Step 4

Don’t Ask “Is This Good?”

That question is too early.

Ask this instead:

Is there anything here I can use?

Step 5

Use Placeholders Instead of Stop Signs

You will sometimes reach something you cannot solve while drafting. That does not mean the whole draft needs to stop.

[SOURCE HERE]
[BETTER WORD]
[CHECK MLA]
[EXPLAIN WHY THIS MATTERS]
[CONNECT THIS TO THESIS]
[COME BACK TO THIS]
STOPPING

I cannot remember the author's exact wording, so I stop drafting and spend twenty minutes searching.

KEEPING GOING

Carr argues that [ADD QUOTE HERE], which shows...

A gap in a draft does not have to become a stop sign.
Step 6

What Does Your Reader Need Next?

You have an idea now. Instead of evaluating the whole paper, decide what would help the reader understand that idea.

Step 7

Keep Drafting Before You Start Editing

You now have material. The temptation may be to go back to the first sentence and start polishing it.

Resist that for a little longer.

Your next job:

Keep writing for 10 minutes without editing your opening sentence.
