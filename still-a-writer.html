C1000 / C1000E Writing Lab

🌱 Still a Writer

Writing can get personal. This activity helps you separate a rough writing moment from a story about who you are.

No points. No perfect answers. Just a reset you can actually use.

Step 1 of 5 Meet the writers
Step 1 · Meet the Writers

Someone Here Might Feel Familiar

Choose one student whose situation feels interesting or recognizable. You are not choosing who is “most struggling.” You are choosing a story to explore.

Step 2 · Change the Story

What Would You Tell a Classmate?

If this student were sitting next to you, which response would actually help?

❌ Story
🔎 Evidence
Recovery thought: It does not pretend everything is fine. It separates what happened from what happens next.
Step 3 · Your Turn

Which Thought Has Shown Up for You?

Select any that feel familiar. This is not a quiz, and nothing here is a diagnosis.

What actually happened underneath that story?

❌ Big conclusion Choose a thought above.
🔎 What actually happened Choose the closest evidence.
Step 4 · Build a Writer Reminder

Choose Something True Enough to Believe

A useful writer reminder does not have to sound inspirational. It just has to be credible enough to help you move.

Pick the one you can actually believe today.

You do not need the most positive sentence. You need a sentence that interrupts the big conclusion without pretending the writing moment is easy.

Step 5 · Choose a Next Move

What Would Help Most Right Now?

Your reminder changes the story. Your next move changes what happens next.

You only need one next move.

Choose the action that would make the situation a little more workable—not the action that proves you can handle everything alone.

Step 6 · Your Reset

Still a Writer

Your card does not promise that writing will be easy. It reminds you what you can do when writing gets hard.

One more thing:

Asking for help is part of doing college. It is not evidence that you cannot do college.

You're still a writer when writing is going badly.

College writers get confused. They get feedback. They miss things. They revise. They ask questions. They start again. The goal is not to never struggle. The goal is to know what you can do when struggle shows up.
