C1000 / C1000E Revision Lab

🎬 The Editing Room

Turn Feedback Into a Better Draft. Reading comments is only the beginning. Your job is to figure out what the reader noticed, what kind of work your draft needs, and what to change first.

You are still the writer. Feedback gives you information. You make the editorial decisions.

Scene 1 · Translate the Comment

What Is the Reader Actually Asking For?

Feedback often arrives in professor-language or peer-review-language. Before revising, turn the comment into a job you understand.

💬 INSTRUCTOR COMMENT
“Can you develop this idea further?”

What revision job does this point toward?

💬 READER COMMENT
“I'm not seeing how this evidence supports your claim.”

Translate that into a revision task.

Editing-room move: Turn “What does this comment mean?” into “What do I need to do?”

Scene 3 · Don't Polish a Scene You Might Cut

You Have 30 Minutes. What Comes First?

Your draft has seven comments: the thesis doesn't match the essay; one paragraph doesn't support the argument; one quotation needs explanation; the conclusion needs development; one comma splice; one MLA error; one typo.

Where should you begin?

Revision isn't about completing the most comments. It's about making the changes that improve the draft most.
Scene 4 · The Domino Effect

One Big Edit Can Change the Whole Draft

💬 INSTRUCTOR COMMENT
“Your thesis says social media is harmful, but most of your essay argues that its effects depend on how people use it.”

What should you examine first?

🎯 Revise thesis→✓ Paragraph 1 still works→🔧 Paragraph 2 needs a new topic sentence→✓ Paragraph 3 fits better→🔧 Update conclusion
Big edits create ripples. That is why writers usually make higher-level decisions before polishing sentences.
Scene 5 · You're Still the Editor

Feedback Is Information, Not an Order

💬 PEER REVIEW COMMENT
“You should delete this whole paragraph.”

What is the strongest response?

💬 TWO REVIEWERS
Reviewer A: “I want more background here.”
Reviewer B: “The introduction feels too long.”

Those comments conflict. Now what?

Don't just count votes. Ask what reader experience might be underneath the comments.
Final Scene · Your Draft

Turn Your Feedback Into an Editing Plan

Paste up to five comments from your own draft. You don't need to enter all your feedback—start with the comments that seem most important or confusing.

🎬 My Editing Plan

Work from bigger decisions toward smaller ones. You can change this order if your draft needs something different—you're the editor.

Don't polish a scene you might cut.

You don't have to “fix the whole paper.” Make the next useful change, then look again.
