3-minute academic reset

Reset Before You Start

Sometimes you open a reading, assignment, or draft and your brain immediately says: “Nope.”

You do not have to feel perfectly calm before you begin. Give yourself a few minutes to settle, reduce the pressure, and choose one manageable next move.

You are in charge of this reset.
Skip anything that feels uncomfortable or unhelpful. There is no perfect breathing pattern, posture, or way to calm down. The goal is simply to become a little more ready to work.

Step 1

Notice What Is Happening

You do not need to fix anything yet. Which description is closest to what you are experiencing?

Step 2

Come Back to Where You Are

Look away from the assignment for a moment. Without trying too hard, notice:

  1. 3 things you can see
  2. 2 things you can physically feel — your feet on the floor, the chair underneath you, your hands, the temperature
  3. 1 sound you can hear

You do not have to write them down. Just notice that you are here, in this room, at this moment.

Step 3

Release a Little Tension

Check three places where people often hold tension:

  • Let your shoulders drop if they are raised.
  • Unclench your jaw if it is tight.
  • Let your hands rest instead of gripping.
If comfortable, try two or three slower breaths.

Let the exhale be a little longer than the inhale. For example: breathe in gently, then breathe out slowly.

If focusing on breathing makes you uncomfortable, skip this part and simply keep noticing your feet, chair, or surroundings.

Step 4

Take the Assignment Down to Size

Anxiety can turn:

“I have something difficult to do.”

into

“I have to solve all of this right now.”

You do not need to finish the reading or paper right now. You only need a next move.
