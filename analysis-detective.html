Case 1 · Something Doesn't Match

Find the Clue

Your job is not to summarize the whole passage. Find one detail that gives you something to investigate.

Maya checked her phone again. No messages. She placed it facedown on the table, picked it up thirty seconds later, and refreshed the screen. Across the restaurant, her friends were laughing. Maya smiled at something someone said, although she had not heard it.

Case 2 · Why That Word?

Investigate the Language

This time, the clue is not an event. It is a word choice.

A delivery company describes its drivers as “independent partners” who have the freedom to choose when they work. Drivers use their own cars, pay for their own gas, and receive no guaranteed number of hours each week.

Which phrase deserves investigation?

What could that phrase be doing?

Detective Rule: More than one interpretation can be defensible. What matters is whether you can connect it back to evidence.
Case 3 · What's Missing?

Investigate the Gap

Sometimes the most revealing clue is an assumption the writer never says out loud.

College students should work no more than fifteen hours per week. Students who work less have more time to study, attend office hours, and participate in campus life. Colleges should encourage all students to reduce their work schedules during the semester.
