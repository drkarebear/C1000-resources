🕵️ The Hidden Curriculum

Students can be capable of the thinking we want and still miss expectations we never made visible. Your task: uncover the academic moves hiding inside ordinary assignment language.

Raccoon Check 🦝

What would a student have to already know in order to follow this direction?

Learn Why This Works

Transparent teaching does not mean doing the thinking for students. It means making the task, purpose, criteria, and academic moves easier to see so students can direct their effort toward the learning itself.

What counts as hidden curriculum?

Hidden curriculum includes expectations that experienced college readers and writers may recognize automatically but that are not actually explained: what “analyze” requires, what makes a source scholarly, how evidence connects to a claim, what substantial revision looks like, or what successful participation sounds like.

Does explaining the moves lower rigor?

No. Showing students what a move is does not perform the move for them. A model, checklist, annotated example, or brief explanation can make expectations visible while leaving students responsible for interpretation, judgment, and composition.

What is the practical test?

Ask: “Could a capable student misunderstand what I mean because the academic move is named but not shown?” If yes, there may be hidden curriculum worth making visible.
