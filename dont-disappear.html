5–10 minute re-entry tool

Don’t Disappear

Maybe you missed a deadline. Maybe you stopped checking Canvas. Maybe you got confused, fell behind, or felt embarrassed about coming back.

You do not have to solve the whole semester right now. First, find out what is actually true and make one useful move back into the course.

Falling behind and deciding what to do next are two different things.
Avoiding the course cannot tell you what your options are. Checking can.

Step 1

What Is Your Brain Telling You?

Choose the thought that sounds closest to what is making it hard to come back.

Pick the closest one. You do not have to prove it is true.

Step 2

Separate the Facts From the Story

Your brain may be filling in missing information with conclusions. Try separating what happened from what you think it means.

FACTI missed two assignments.
STORYThere is no point coming back now.
Unknown is not the same as bad news.
Questions about late work, grades, or what your professor recommends are still questions—not answers.

Step 3

Orient First. Catch Up Second.

Do not review the entire semester right now. Spend about three minutes checking only what you need to re-enter.

Open Canvas and check four things:
  1. What is happening right now in the course?
  2. What is the next upcoming deadline?
  3. Is there an announcement, grade comment, or message I need to see?
  4. Is there one missed item I need to ask about?

You do not have to solve anything yet. Just find your current location.

Step 4

Sort What You Found

Everything does not need the same kind of attention.

DO NOWCurrent work that is still open and actionable.
ASK ABOUTMissing work, unclear policies, or something you cannot figure out alone.
LET GO FOR NOWSomething you cannot change today or do not need to solve before re-entering.

Step 5

Re-Enter Before You Catch Up

“I cannot participate again until I finish everything I missed.”

That can feel logical, but re-entering the course and catching up are not always the same task.

Sometimes the strongest move is to start participating in what is happening now while separately figuring out what can still be recovered from the past.

Re-entry is a different goal from being fully caught up. You are trying to reconnect with the course first.

Step 6

Ask One Clear Question

If you need information from your professor, you do not need to write a long apology. Give enough context, show what you have already checked, and ask one specific question.

A useful structure:

Context → What I’ve already checked or done → One clear question

Hi Professor,

I’m working on getting re-engaged with the course. I see that I missed the draft due Friday, and I’ve checked Canvas and started this week’s work. Could you let me know whether I should still submit the missed draft or focus on the next assignment?

Thank you.

Step 7

Choose Your Re-Entry Move

Pick one action that can move you from “avoiding the course” to “participating again.”

Your plan

My Re-Entry Card

You do not need a perfect recovery plan. You need a next move that is real enough to do.

What my brain was saying
What actually happened
What I can do now
What I need to ask
What I am not solving today
My next move
You’re back.

Not caught up. Not finished. Back.

Now do the next move on your card.
