Perfectionism + Submission Lab

📤 Submit the Imperfect Thing

Sometimes the choice is not “perfect paper” versus “bad paper.” It is “submit the best version I have” versus “keep polishing until I miss the chance to submit.”

This lab helps you tell the difference between a draft that needs one useful fix and a draft that is already good enough to turn in.

Part 1 of 8Catch the perfectionism thought
Part 1 · Catch It

“It’s Not Good Enough Yet”

Your essay is due in 18 minutes. It has a clear thesis, body paragraphs, evidence, and a conclusion that feels rushed.
Your brain: “If I submit this and it isn’t polished, everyone will see I’m a bad writer.”

Which response checks the thought without pretending the paper is perfect?

CBT move: replace all-or-nothing thinking with a more accurate question: “Is this complete enough to be assessed?”
Part 2 · Assess It

Imperfect Is Not the Same as Unsubmit-able

✅ Core taskAnswers the prompt
✅ Main structureHas thesis + developed body
⚠️ PolishConclusion is rushed; a few sentences are awkward

What is the best classification?

Ask in this order:
1. Did I answer the task?
2. Is the main work there?
3. Is there one quick fix that changes meaning or clarity?
4. If not, submit.
Part 3 · Last Useful Fix

Not Every Improvement Is Equally Important

You have 10 minutes left.

Which change is worth doing first?

Adult-learning move: prioritize the change with the biggest effect on meaning, task completion, or readability—not the change that makes the paper feel more “finished.”
Part 4 · Trap

Polishing Can Become Avoidance

You have rewritten the first sentence of your introduction six times. The current sentence is clear and accurate.

What is the most useful next move?

Perfectionism can disguise itself as productivity. You are technically “working” while avoiding the more important decision: Is this ready enough to submit?
Part 5 · Deadline Challenge

What Would You Do With the Last 12 Minutes?

Your draft:
• Answers the prompt
• Has a thesis
• Uses two required sources
• One body paragraph is shorter than you wanted
• Two MLA citations need a quick check
• The conclusion exists but is basic

Pick the strongest plan.

Submission strategy: protect the work you already did. A minor weakness does not need to become a missed submission.
Part 6 · Outcome Check

Which Risk Is Bigger?

Option A
Submit a complete but imperfect essay.

Possible outcome: partial or strong credit + feedback.
Option B
Keep polishing, miss the deadline, submit nothing.

Possible outcome: zero, late penalty, or no feedback.

What is the more accurate question?

CBT move: shift from “What if they judge this?” to “What action best serves my actual goal right now?”
Part 7 · Your Turn

Run a Submission Check

Important: If a required part is genuinely missing, this lab is not telling you to ignore it. The skill is distinguishing required work from endless polishing.
Part 8 · Submit

Protect the Work You Did
