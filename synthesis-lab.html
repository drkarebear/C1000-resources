🧩 Synthesis Lab: Get the Sources Talking

Three sources in one paragraph do not automatically equal synthesis.

🚨 SOURCE PARADE DETECTED.
The sources are standing next to each other. The writer has not yet shown us what relationship matters.
🔓 Unwritten Rule: Synthesis means organizing sources around an idea, question, relationship, or claim that you control.

🧩 Synthesis Check

1. What idea is this paragraph developing?

2. Which sources actually belong in this conversation?

3. How do they relate?

4. What do I notice because I put them together?

5. Why does that matter to my claim?

🧩 Conversation Built

Synthesis is not squeezing more sources into a paragraph. It is showing the reader what you notice when ideas are placed in conversation.

🧩 I Have Multiple Sources. Now What?

1. What idea is this paragraph trying to develop?

2. Which sources actually help with that idea?

3. How do those sources relate?

4. What do I notice because I put them together?

🧠 A synthesis sentence starter can help—but it is scaffolding, not a law.

“Taken together, these sources suggest...”
“While both sources identify..., they differ in...”
“Source B complicates Source A by...”
“Viewed together, these findings raise a question about...”

🔗 Build One Synthesis Move From Your Own Sources

You do not need to draft a whole paragraph here. Bring in two sources from a project you are actually working on and make one relationship visible.
