ENGL C1000 · Editing & Proofreading

🩺 Proofreading ER

Something looks off. Diagnose it before you fix it.

PATIENT ARRIVING:

“The article raises an important concern however the writer does not explain what should happen next.”

The temptation is to start throwing commas at the sentence.

🔓 Unwritten Rule: Good proofreaders do not just hunt for “errors.” They identify what kind of problem they are looking at, decide whether it actually needs attention, and choose a revision that preserves the writer’s meaning.
Your job in this ER: Diagnose → Treat → Recheck.

🏥 ER Departments

🧱 Boundaries
Where does one complete thought end?

🔍 Clarity
Can the reader tell what the sentence means?

⚙️ Consistency
Do tense, agreement, and point of view stay steady?

🚦 Punctuation
Do the marks help the reader follow the structure?

💬 Quotations
Is borrowed language integrated clearly?

🔤 Words
Spelling, usage, capitalization, confused words.

🧭 Connections
Is the relationship between ideas clear?

🩺 Patient Stable

Good proofreading is not finding the most errors. It is noticing what your reader needs and making purposeful choices about what to change.

🔎 Quick Proofreading Triage

🧰 60-Second Proofreading Check

1. Read the sentence for meaning first.
2. Is anything actually unclear?
3. What type of issue is it?
4. Is it a one-time issue or a pattern?
5. What is the smallest useful revision?
6. Read the revised sentence again: did the meaning survive?
