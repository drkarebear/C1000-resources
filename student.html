For students

What do you need right now?

You do not need to know the name of the writing skill. Start with the sentence that sounds most like what is happening.

These are short tools, not a sequence. Pick one that fits the moment you are in. You can come back and try something different later.

Jump to what sounds familiar

📖

I’m stuck on the reading.

The words are there, but meaning, purpose, or direction is not. ⌄
Try this if…Wait, What Did I Just Read?A sentence or paragraph stops making sense. Practice chunking, paraphrasing, finding the actor and action, and rereading strategically. Try this if…Read Like You Have a PurposeYou can read the page, but you are not sure what deserves your attention. Learn to read and annotate for a specific job. Try this if…Active ReadingYou keep reaching the bottom of the page without much sticking. Practice doing something with the text as you read. Try this if…What Does “Analyze” Mean?Your assignment says “analyze,” but that instruction still feels vague. Turn the word into concrete moves you can make. Try this if…What Does “Reflect” Mean?You are being asked to reflect and are not sure how that differs from summary or opinion. Try this if…Decode the AssignmentThe prompt itself feels like the hardest reading. Break it into the task, requirements, choices, and evidence of success.
💡

I understand it, but I don’t know what to say.

Move from understanding a text to having an idea worth writing about. ⌄
Try this if…The Analysis DetectiveYou can summarize the reading, but “What do you think about it?” makes your mind go blank. Move from clue → pattern → interpretation → significance. Try this if…Make Your CaseYou have an opinion or idea but need to turn it into an arguable claim you can actually support. Try this if…Build a Working ThesisYour thesis feels too broad, obvious, or hard to defend. Practice making it more focused and useful. Try this if…Show Me the ConnectionYou included evidence, but the reader still has to guess why it proves your point. Practice claim → evidence → reasoning. Try this if…Develop, Don’t PadA paragraph feels too short, but adding more words is not making it stronger. Practice developing the thinking through explanation, specificity, evidence, and reasoning. Try this if…Rhetorical ChoicesYou need to explain how a writer’s choices affect readers, meaning, or purpose—not just identify a technique. Try this if…Stop Performing “Academic”You know what you mean, but you are making the sentence harder because you think college writing has to sound complicated.
✍️

I have to write, but I can’t get started.

Lower the starting friction without lowering the work. ⌄
Try this if…Good Enough to DraftYou are trying to make the first sentence good before you have allowed yourself to discover the idea. Try this if…Writer’s Block BusterYou are staring at the page and need a concrete way to generate movement. Try this if…Focus RescueYou know what you need to do but cannot get your attention to stay with it. Match the kind of stuck to a small strategy you can test. Try this if…Reset Before You StartYour body and brain feel too activated to begin reading or writing. Use a short reset before asking yourself to focus. Try this if…Study LabYou want to figure out which conditions actually help you work. Notice, predict, test, and adjust instead of searching for one perfect study method. Try this if…The Writing ProcessThe assignment feels like one giant task. See writing as a set of different jobs you can do at different moments.
🔎

Research and sources are overwhelming.

Find, understand, combine, and use sources without letting them take over the paper. ⌄
Try this if…Research Without DrowningYou have tabs, articles, and search results everywhere and need a way to decide what to do next. Try this if…Source IntegrationQuotes or paraphrases feel dropped into the paragraph. Practice setting up, using, and explaining source material. Try this if…Synthesis LabYou have multiple sources but are discussing them one at a time. Practice putting sources into conversation around an idea. Try this if…MLA ReviewYou need a focused check of common MLA choices before submitting research-based writing.
🔧

I have a draft. Now what?

Figure out what kind of revision the draft actually needs before polishing sentences. ⌄
Try this if…The Editing RoomYou received feedback and are not sure what to change first. Sort comments into rethink, build, move/cut, connect, and polish. Try this if…Professor Comment DecoderA comment such as “develop,” “unclear,” or “analyze more” leaves you unsure what action to take. Try this if…Reverse OutlineYour draft exists, but you cannot tell whether the organization actually works. Map what each paragraph is doing now. Try this if…Peer ReviewYou need to give or use peer feedback in a way that goes beyond proofreading someone else’s sentences. Try this if…Proofreading ERThe ideas and organization are mostly settled and it is time to hunt for high-impact sentence-level problems. Try this if…MechanicsYou want targeted practice with common grammar, punctuation, spelling, or sentence-level issues. Try this if…Submit the Imperfect ThingThe deadline is close and you are caught in endless polishing. Decide whether the work is complete enough to be assessed and choose the last useful fix.
🧠

My brain is making this harder right now.

Sometimes the obstacle is not only a writing skill. Find a way back into the work. ⌄
Try this if…Still a WriterA difficult assignment, grade, or comment is turning into “Maybe I’m just bad at writing.” Separate one writing moment from your identity as a writer. Try this if…You Belong in the ProcessQuestions, revision, feedback, or needing help are making you wonder whether you are doing college “wrong.” See what normal college participation actually looks like. Try this if…You’re Seeing Their Highlight ReelSomeone else seems faster, smarter, or more prepared and your brain is turning that visible moment into a verdict about you. Try this if…What If I Ask a Dumb Question?You need clarification, but you are worried asking will make you look unprepared. Turn the fear into a specific, useful question. Try this if…Stop Performing “Academic”You are using complicated language because you are afraid ordinary, precise language will not sound smart enough. Try this if…Reset Before You StartStress is taking up the attention you need for the task. Use a brief educational reset, then return to one manageable next step.
😬

I’m behind, discouraged, or something went wrong.

Work with what is still possible instead of disappearing from the course. ⌄
Try this if…Don’t DisappearYou missed work, stopped checking the class, or feel embarrassed about coming back. Find one concrete way to reconnect. Try this if…What’s Still Possible?You are looking at everything that has gone wrong and need to identify the work or choices that can still change the outcome. Try this if…One Grade Isn’t the StoryOne score is starting to feel like proof of how the whole course—or your ability—will turn out. Try this if…Essay TriageYou do not have time to fix everything. Identify the changes most likely to protect meaning, task completion, and readability. Try this if…Submit the Imperfect ThingPerfectionism is threatening to turn a completed-enough assignment into a missing assignment.
🙋

I need help, but I’m not sure what to do.

Figure out who to ask, what to ask, and how to keep ownership of the work. ⌄
Try this if…Who Do I Ask?You know you need support but are not sure whether the right person is your professor, a tutor, counselor, librarian, or someone else. Try this if…What If I Ask a Dumb Question?You have a question but fear is keeping you from asking it. Separate what you know from what you are predicting and make the question specific. Try this if…Crack the Academic CodeA professor or assignment uses academic language you technically recognize, but you still do not know what kind of thinking or writing it expects.
A useful question does not hand your thinking to someone else. It helps you get the information, clarification, or feedback you need to make the next decision yourself.

Not sure where to start?

Choose the problem that is stopping you from making progress today. You are not choosing a permanent label—you are choosing one next move.
