ENGL C1000 · The unwritten rules

🧠 What Does “Analyze” Mean?

Your professor keeps saying it. Let's make it visible.

Your assignment says ANALYZE. Your rubric says ANALYSIS. Your professor writes MORE ANALYSIS. 😐 Okay. What are you actually supposed to DO?
🔓 Unwritten Rule: “Analyze” isn't one mysterious action. It's a collection of thinking moves.

🧠 Writer Detected

Analysis becomes visible when your reader can follow what you noticed, what you think it might mean, how it connects, and why it matters.

🧠 The Analysis Check

🔍 What do I notice? Point to something specific.
🧩 What might it mean? Interpret rather than only report.
🔗 How does it connect? Link it to your claim, a pattern, another idea, or context.
💡 Why does it matter? Help your reader see the significance.
You don't need every move every time. These are options when your reader needs to see more of your thinking.

🎰 Thinking Starter Machine

Press the button when you need a way back into your thinking.

Try One Move on Your Own Work

This part is not scored. Choose one detail, quotation, statistic, example, or pattern from something you are reading or writing now. Then make one piece of your reasoning visible.

That's analysis: not sounding mysterious, but letting a reader follow a piece of your thinking.
