Belonging + Social Comparison Lab

👀 You’re Seeing Their Highlight Reel

You see someone else’s answer, grade, confidence, or polished draft. You usually do not see everything that happened before it.

This activity helps you notice comparison thoughts, check what evidence you actually have, and shift attention back to your own next useful move.

Part 1 of 8Catch the comparison
Part 1 · Catch It

The Comparison Happens Fast

A student in your class gives a thoughtful answer about the reading.
Your brain: “They just understand this stuff. I’m the one who doesn’t belong here.”

Which response is most useful?

CBT move: separate what you observed from the story your mind built around it.
Part 2 · Evidence

What Did You Actually See?

Observation: A classmate turns in a polished-looking draft before class.

Which statement stays closest to the evidence?

Observation: “They have a polished draft.”
Interpretation: “Writing is easy for them.”
Prediction: “I’ll never catch up.”
Part 3 · Behind the Scene

The Finished Moment Hides the Process

WHAT YOU SEE:
A student gives a confident answer in discussion.
They reread the passage twice, wrote a note in the margin, and asked a classmate whether they were understanding it correctly.
WHAT YOU SEE:
A student earns a high grade on an essay.
They rewrote the thesis, went to tutoring, cut a paragraph that was not working, and asked the professor one question about the prompt.
WHAT YOU SEE:
A student submits the assignment early.
Their work schedule changed that week, so they used one free afternoon to finish early because they knew the weekend would be difficult.
The point is not that everyone is secretly struggling. The point is that you usually do not have enough information to turn someone else’s visible moment into a verdict about your own ability.
Part 4 · Thought Check

Which Thinking Trap Showed Up?

You hear two classmates talking about how they already finished the reading. You are only halfway through.
Your brain: “Everyone is ahead of me. I’m always the last one to get things done.”

What is happening in that thought?

Watch for: “everyone,” “always,” “never,” mind-reading, and treating one visible moment as proof of a permanent ability difference.
Part 5 · Fair Comparison

You’re Comparing Their Outside to Your Inside

What you know about them

Their answer.
Their finished draft.
The grade they mentioned.
The confidence you saw.

What you know about yourself

Your confusion.
Your false starts.
Your work schedule.
Your worry.
Every sentence you deleted.

Why is that comparison uneven?

Fairer question: “What can I learn from what I’m seeing without using it as evidence against myself?”
Part 6 · Turn Comparison Into Information

Comparison Can Point to a Strategy

A classmate says they go to the Writing Center before major essays. Your first thought is: “Of course they do well. They probably know what they’re doing.”

What is a more useful response?

Adult-learning move: use other people as sources of possible strategies, not as measuring sticks for your worth.
Part 7 · Your Turn

Rewrite the Comparison Without Lying to Yourself

The goal is not positive thinking. A useful replacement thought should be believable, evidence-based, and leave room for action.
Part 8 · Return to Your Lane

What Can You Do With the Information?

Choose one move that brings your attention back to your own learning.
